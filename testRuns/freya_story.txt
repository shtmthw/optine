Freya had always believed that love was a song someone else had to write for her. The melody she was meant to dance to was her own, and she never let anyone else choose the tempo.

When Matthew entered her life, he was everything she never thought she needed. Charming, witty, and effortlessly bright, he tried to fill the empty corners of her heart with his laughter. But Freya felt an emptiness that no one could soothe.

“Hey Freya, want to grab coffee?” Matthew asked, his smile wide, his eyes searching hers for that spark he thought existed. She smiled back, polite but distant, and nodded.

The coffee shop buzzed with life, but her mind wandered elsewhere. She was not ready to surrender her song to someone else's rhythm. She wanted a love that felt as wild and untamed as the wind she rode her bike on.

When the day came, she thanked him kindly, and quietly walked away, her steps echoing her own heartbeats. She was the conductor of her own story, and that story had no room for Matthew.

Freya learned that true affection starts with acceptance – first of oneself, then of another’s place in the grand orchestra of life.
